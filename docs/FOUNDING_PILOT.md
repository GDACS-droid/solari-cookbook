# Founding pilot — prepared, not sent

AcreBrief can be sold now as a concierge investigation service without claiming that the Lee Clerk filing feed is live.

## Offer

**Founding design-partner pilot: $750–$1,500/month.** A $500 two-week paid beta is an acceptable low-friction validation offer.

The customer supplies a buy box: ZIPs, property type, price/value range, strategy, and disqualifiers. AcreBrief returns a small daily/weekly set of newly changed Cape Coral/Lee properties, the official-source evidence, a property timeline, unresolved facts, and a concise “why investigate” explanation. The output is the service; the customer is not buying raw government data.

Truthful positioning:

> AcreBrief monitors municipal and property records and investigates emerging distress signals across Cape Coral and Lee County. We are onboarding a separate direct Clerk filing feed that, if licensed for this use, will add newly filed foreclosure cases to the same property briefs.

Do not say the current product monitors new court foreclosure filings. Do not promise outreach data, clear title, willingness to sell, equity, or a complete lien search.

## Prepared email

**Subject:** Cape Coral / Lee County property-change pilot

> Hi — I’m building AcreBrief, an evidence-first property intelligence service for acquisition teams in Southwest Florida.
>
> Instead of handing you another static lead list, AcreBrief monitors official municipal and property sources, detects what changed, resolves the event to a parcel, and returns a short source-backed investigation brief. The live Cape Coral path currently covers code/foreclosure-registration changes, utility-lien events, permits, municipal payoff state for selected parcels, and Florida property-roll evidence. A direct Lee Clerk filing feed is a separate licensing step and is not represented as live today.
>
> I’m looking for a small number of founding design partners. If you share a real buy box, I’ll deliver a two-week concierge brief and measure whether it saves research time or finds better investigations. Would a 15-minute workflow conversation be useful?

Send only after the owner approves the target and channel. Use a person’s public business contact route; do not enrich private contact data or automate a sequence.

## Discovery questions and proof

1. Show me the last property your team rejected after spending too long across county systems.
2. Which source changes cause immediate action, and which produce noise?
3. What minimum evidence must be present before a researcher spends 15 minutes?
4. How do you define the buy box and disqualifiers today?
5. What does a credible daily brief replace in time or vendor spend?
6. Would you pay $500 for a two-week concierge test using your real buy box?

Record only observed answers and real workflow outcomes: briefs delivered, opens, evidence clicks, investigations accepted/rejected, research minutes saved, offers/contracts if voluntarily reported, and paid amount. Never fabricate testimonials, conversion, or ROI.
